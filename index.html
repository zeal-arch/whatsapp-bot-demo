The WhatsApp → ERP bot

30-second answer: the project manager is acting as a human data-entry layer between WhatsApp and the ERP. The bot turns every text, voice note and bill photo into a record (who, what item, how many, rate, amount, which project). It posts on its own only when it is confident, sends the rest to a one-tap review queue, and never guesses on money.

Where confidence comes from (strongest first): numbers that agree with each other (items add up, bill total = voice amount) → lookups (vendor known, price in normal range) → the model's own confidence, last.

Cost, law, testing

  

    
Cost
About ₹1–2 of AI cost per voice note or bill at list prices (Sarvam speech ₹30/hour). Cheap classifier first; vision model only for bills. Rough estimate, not measured.

    
DPDP law
Rules notified Nov 2025. Consent managers from 13 Nov 2026; main duties (notice, consent, breach reporting, deletion) from 13 May 2027. Tell group members a bot is present.

    
Testing
Label 200–300 real messages from 3–4 projects. Measure each field (amount, qty, vendor, project). Split by project. Start in shadow mode; switch on auto-post one category at a time.

  







  
ERP, SaaS and APIs in plain words

  

    
ERP
Enterprise Resource Planning
One central system where a firm records its operations: projects, purchases, stock, vendors, money. One purchase entry updates project cost, vendor balance, stock and accounts together.
India: Tally, Zoho Books, Odoo, SAP Business One; construction: Onsite, Powerplay, RDash. Many small firms still use Excel + WhatsApp + Tally.

    
SaaS
Software as a Service
Online software paid monthly. Data is on the provider's cloud; they do updates. Inside: screens → backend rules → database. Multi-tenant: many firms share one system but each sees only its own data, so every record has a company id and a project id.

Lessons from others

  

    
Angi / HomeAdvisor
Sold the same lead to many pros. The FTC ordered up to $7.2M (2023) over lead quality. In 2025 Angi let homeowners choose instead. Lead quality is the product.

    
AI receptionists (US)
Avoca ($1B valuation, 2026), Hatch (bought by Yelp ~$270M, 2026): answer every enquiry fast and qualify it. Speed matters, but the “21x in 5 minutes” figure is a 2007 industry study; don't quote it as research.

    
Indian CRMs
TeleCRM (~₹799/user/month), LeadSquared, Kylas, Sell.do: built for real estate. None publishes proof its AI scoring works. No interiors-specific tool found.

Pain points

  

    
The advance trap
Low quote → advance paid → price rises (e.g. ₹8–10L becomes ₹18L) → delays → no refund. Consumer courts have ordered refunds.

    
Quotes can't be compared
Different materials and units. firstbriq's own article: quotes from ₹8L to ₹30L for the same 2BHK.

    
Trust and quality
Portfolios can't be verified; material swaps; counterfeit plywood reported by brands.

    
Coordination chaos
Many vendors and trades, all on WhatsApp, nothing recorded. Your round-1 problem.

    
Labour
Shortage of trained workers (~2 million estimated); dependence on a few carpenters.

    
Payments and GST
Large advances, unclear milestones, cash and handwritten bills.
