# Home interiors, home improvement and adjacent companies: global and India comparison for firstbriq

Research date: 21 October 2026. Public web pages only. Many figures come from third-party aggregators (Capterra, GetApp, CB Insights, blogs); these are marked "third-party" or "unverified". Company claims are marked "company claim". India entries cover offering, model and positioning only. India financials are left to another researcher.

firstbriq reference point (from the brief, not re-verified): Bengaluru-only marketplace for verified interior designers. It has structured profiles (minimum budget), milestone payments, warranties, AI review summaries, WhatsApp guides, and a "Studio" workspace (moodboards, decor task board, vendor quote comparison, AI copilot). It also onboards designers as vendors.

## Q1. Global marketplaces and discovery: what they offer, how they make money, what worked and what failed

### Takeaway
The lead-sale model (Angi, Thumbtack, Bark, MyBuilder) makes money at scale but loses pros' trust. Angi gave up blasting one request to many pros in January 2025 and lost most of its network revenue. The models that hold up are paid membership with strict vetting and a platform-backed guarantee (Checkatrade, hipages), or a small transaction fee with deposit protection (Sweeten). Managed, venture-funded online interior design (Modsy, Laurel & Wolf, Homepolish) failed several times. Each time it was the same mix: high-touch service, thin margins, and dependence on the next funding round or a buyer. Designers and clients were left unpaid or stranded.

### Inferences
- Angi's 2025 change is the clearest evidence that blasting one request to many pros hurts both sides. firstbriq should keep homeowner-chosen, limited introductions and should not sell the same lead to several designers.
- Pro anger at pay per lead (Bark, Thumbtack, Angi) comes from paying for leads that do not convert, with refunds that are hard to get. A subscription with clear lead-quality rules (hipages, Checkatrade) or a fee only on a signed project (Sweeten, BuildZoom) puts the pros' and the platform's interests in line.
- Guarantees work as a trust signal only when they are capped, well defined and actually honoured. Angi faces a class action, and Thumbtack users report denials. A small, clear guarantee firstbriq can pay out reliably is better than a large one it cannot.
- The online-design failures show that a platform which collects money and then fails leaves both designers and homeowners exposed. Holding money in escrow or a third-party bank account protects both sides even if firstbriq itself gets into trouble.

### Gaps
- Thumbtack's official 2025–26 revenue and profitability: no primary source found; the figures circulating are third-party.
- Houzz Pro's current official prices: the pricing page could not be fetched (503/404), and third-party figures conflict.
- Hearth's financials and RenoFi's current status (layoffs, latest round): nothing reliable found.
- Bark ownership and revenue: not researched.

## Q2. Software for interior and renovation professionals: features, AI and pricing

### Takeaway
Software for interior designers costs about $35–$130 per user per month (Programa, Mydoma/Studio Designer, DesignFiles, Houzz Pro entry). Contractor and builder platforms cost far more and often hide their prices (Buildertrend, Procore). The common features are client portals, product sourcing/clipping, proposals, invoicing, moodboards and 3D. AI is being added for takeoffs, estimates, schedules and connections to outside AI tools (MCP). Two consolidations stand out: Studio Designer and Mydoma merged, and Houzz renamed its AI suite.

### Cited Findings
- **Programa** (Australia, interior designers): $59 per user per month for the first 3 users, $29 for each extra user, and 20% off on annual billing; the Australian price is $49. — [Programa pricing](https://programa.design/pricing); [Programa AU pricing](https://programa.design/au/pricing)
- **Studio Designer / Mydoma Studio**: merged in 2024 and now operate under the Mydoma Studio brand, with a reported $79–129 a month (according to Programa, a competitor). Mydoma itself is listed from $64 per user per month (GetApp, Software Advice); another review gives $58 a month on an annual plan plus a $300 onboarding fee, and a free single-project plan. It has a client portal for viewing progress, signing contracts and approving designs. — [Programa blog (competitor source)](https://programa.design/blog/interior-design-project-management-software); [GetApp](https://www.getapp.co.uk/software/2059074/mydoma-studio); [Tools for Humans](https://www.toolsforhumans.ai/ai-tools/mydoma-studio)
- **DesignFiles**: e-Design plan $55 a month (unlimited projects, moodboards, floor plans, 3D renders, client portal, contracts) and Full Service $75 a month, with a 90-day free trial (third-party). Capterra lists a $35 flat-rate start (unverified). — [ToolRadar](https://toolradar.com/tools/designfiles/pricing); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/179202/DesignFiles/)
- **Houzz Pro**: covered in Q1. AI 2D-to-3D floor plans, AI takeoffs, AI estimates; schedules generated from estimates; advertising sold separately. — [Construction Dive](https://www.constructiondive.com/press-release/20250415-houzz-pro-unveils-ai-powered-3d-floor-plan-features-to-enhance-the-pre-cons-1); [Visualping change log](https://visualping.io/pages/houzz-pricing-alerts-4893953)
- **JobTread** (remodelers and builders): $199 a month base plus $20 per internal user, all features in one plan, prices public. It positions an open API and an MCP server connection to outside AI platforms as its AI approach. — [JobTread comparison (vendor source)](https://jobtread.com/blog/jobtread-vs-buildertrend-construction-management-software-comparison)
- **Buildertrend**: no public prices; quotes depend on builder type and annual volume. Third-party estimates run from about $299 to $1,099 a month. It describes "AI-powered tools" (the specifics are vendor claims). — [Buildertrend vs JobTread (vendor)](https://buildertrend.com/buildertrend-vs-jobtread/); [Downtobid](https://downtobid.com/blog/buildertrend-pricing)
- **Procore** (commercial builders): an annual fee per product, priced on annual construction volume, with unlimited users. Third-party estimates: about $15k–$30k a year for $10m–$50m of volume, plus $50k–$150k+ to implement. AI: "Procore Helix" intelligence layer; Procore Agents for scheduling, RFIs and submittals; Agent Studio (no-code agent building) slated for late 2025. — [Procore factsheet Nov 2025](https://s21.q4cdn.com/306803720/files/doc_downloads/2025/PCOR-Factsheet-2025-11-05.pdf); [OneCrew](https://www.getonecrew.com/post/procore-pricing); [SoftwareConnect](https://softwareconnect.com/reviews/procore/)
- **Hearth** (Q1): contractor software with financing in the quote. A useful example of a fintech feature built into the workflow. — [Hearth pricing](https://gethearth.com/pricing)

### Inferences
- firstbriq's Studio (moodboards, task board, quote comparison, AI copilot) overlaps features that designer SaaS charges $55–$130 a month for in the US. That suggests designer-side SaaS as a possible revenue line. Indian willingness to pay is likely much lower; no evidence was gathered on this.
- Comparing quotes is valuable mainly when quotes share a structure. Houzz's AI estimates and Kukun's tiered estimates point to structuring line items (labour, materials, finish tier) so they can be compared side by side.
- Modsy's planned SaaS pivot and Houzz's dual model suggest that tools for designers are the stickier, more defensible part of a design marketplace.

### Gaps
- No verified AI-specific pricing for any of these tools (no separate price for Procore's agents; Houzz AI is bundled).
- No Indian-priced designer SaaS was benchmarked (for example, local tools used by Bengaluru studios).
- Studio Designer's standalone current pricing was not confirmed from the vendor.

## Q3. AI design tools: what they do and how designers use them

### Takeaway
Consumer AI room-restyling tools (RoomGPT, Interior AI, Collov) are cheap ($9–$30 credit packs or monthly plans) and good for inspiration and as a way in, but they are small businesses. RoomGPT reported a total revenue of $241k. The professional winners are the platforms where AI sits inside the production workflow: Kujiale/Coohom (Manycore), Foyr, Planner 5D and Houzz Pro. Designers mostly use AI for early concepts and client pitches. Fewer than a third of US design pros used AI tools in 2025, and only 17% of UK respondents said AI made their designs better.

### Cited Findings
- **RoomGPT**: 1.6m+ users, total revenue $241k and total profit $168k; last reported month $23k revenue and $16k profit (from an Acquire.com listing, unaudited). Credit packs are $9 (30), $19 (100) and $29 (200). — [Indie Hackers case study](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/case-study-interior-design-ai-saas-company-that-made-168k-in-total-profit-in-6-months-efff542b1e)
- **Interior AI**: about £15–20 a month; described as stronger than RoomGPT for exterior and architectural renders. No user or revenue data found. — [Lilach Bullock roundup](https://www.lilachbullock.com/ai-tools-interior-designers-mock-up-rooms/)
- **Collov AI**: restyles a photo of an existing room; from $16 a month, no free trial (third-party). **Foyr Neo**: browser-based drafting, 3D modelling and rendering; prices vary by source ($29, $39 or $59 a month starting). **Planner 5D**: free tier, premium $4.99 a month, pro about $33–50 a month (sources conflict). **Spacely AI**: no pricing found. — [SpotSaaS Foyr vs Collov](https://www.spotsaas.com/compare/foyr-neo-vs-collov-ai); [fast.io 2026 roundup](https://www.fast.io/resources/best-ai-interior-design-tools-2026.md); [Dupple](https://dupple.com/learn/best-ai-for-home-design)
- **Designer adoption**: 31% of US design pros use AI tools (Houzz survey of 722 businesses, 2025), saving 3+ hours a week. In a UK survey (Architects' Journal), only 17% said their designs were better because of AI. - **Kujiale / Coohom (Manycore)**: see Q4. 2025 revenue RMB 820m, 96.9% from software subscriptions; gross margin 82.2%. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manycore_Tech); [iTiger](https://www.itiger.com/news/1125056920)

### Inferences
- A standalone consumer "restyle my room" AI is a commodity with low revenue. For firstbriq, AI is better used to cut friction in the decisions that lead to a hire: turning a brief or moodboard into a structured scope, comparing quotes, summarising reviews. Image generation alone is not enough.
- AI renders can set unrealistic expectations unless they are tied to real products and prices. Manycore's success comes from rendering linked to manufacturing and product catalogues.

### Gaps
- No primary data on Interior AI, Spacely or Collov revenue or users.
- No India-specific survey of designers' AI use was found.

## Q4. China and other markets (Manycore/Kujiale, Tubatu, Beike Home, Leroy Merlin): lessons on standardisation and trust

### Takeaway
China shows the furthest-developed versions of two ideas firstbriq is testing. One is standardised design software that is also a sales tool (Kujiale, now listed in Hong Kong and profitable on an adjusted basis). The other is platform-controlled money flow: Tubatu's "装修保" (renovation protection) keeps homeowner funds with a partner bank and releases them stage by stage after inspection. Beike Home shows that even a giant with heavy brokerage traffic finds renovation hard. Its renovation revenue was flat to falling in late 2025 while it narrowed losses. Leroy Merlin adds fixed-price installation packages sold by a retailer, with vetted local artisans.

### Cited Findings
- **Manycore Tech (Kujiale / Coohom)**: listed on HKEX (stock code 68) in April 2026, raising about HK$1.02bn (US$130m) at HK$6.72–7.62 a share. 2025 revenue RMB 820m; gross margin 82.2%; adjusted net profit RMB 57.1m; overseas revenue up 28%; 96.9% of revenue from software subscriptions, mostly Kujiale. In 2023 revenue was RMB 663m, and it was loss-making then. 30% of IPO proceeds go to international expansion. — [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/tech/article/3349471/hangzhou-six-little-dragons-member-manycore-seeks-us130m-hong-kong-ipo); [iTiger, listing debut](https://www.itiger.com/news/1125056920); [etnet IPO page](https://etnet.com.hk/www/eng/stocks/ci_ipo_detail.php?code=00068); [Bamboo Works](https://thebambooworks.com/manycore-makes-second-listing-attempt-as-mood-brightens-for-tech-companies); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manycore_Tech)
- **Tubatu (土巴兔 / To8to)**: founded 2008; a marketplace linking homeowners with renovation companies and designers; backed by 58.com; sought a Hong Kong IPO in 2018. — [ACN Newswire](https://www.acnnewswire.com/press-release/english/44230/tubatu,-china's-no.1-online-home-renovation-platform-may-seek-listing-in-hong-kong-soon-upon-qeeka-home's-ipo)

- **Beike Home / KE Holdings** (renovation and furnishing segment): Q3 2025 revenue about RMB 4.3bn, roughly flat year on year; Q4 2025 RMB 3.6bn, down 12% from RMB 4.1bn, as it deliberately cut back some non-brokerage channels. - **Leroy Merlin**: in France, partner artisans are selected by in-house experts and sign a quality charter (Charte Qualité); installation packages have prices and timelines fixed in advance. In South Africa a services marketplace launched in January 2025: vetted contractors for installation and repair bookings, with upfront pricing. Products carry a 2-year guarantee on top of the legal one. Customer reviews are mixed; one forum complaint says Leroy Merlin adds a markup to artisan quotes. — [macommune.info](https://www.macommune.info/leroy-merlin-vous-accompagne-dans-vos-travaux-grace-a-son-service-dinstallation-a-domicile); [AfriGIS, Jan 2025](https://www.afrigis.co.za/leroy-merlin-partners-with-afrigis-to-build-services-marketplace/); [Que Choisir forum](https://forum.quechoisir.org/post305930.html)

### Inferences
- Tubatu's mechanism (bank-held escrow, release only after a stage is inspected, a freeze when a complaint is raised) is the most complete model for firstbriq's milestone payments. Its 2018 problem shows the funds must sit with a regulated bank or escrow provider, not on the platform's own books. In India that points to an RBI-regulated escrow or payment-aggregator setup; this was not researched here.
- Manycore shows that design software can be a high-margin, profitable subscription when it is tied to manufacturing and catalogues. It is a long-term option for firstbriq's Studio if it becomes the designers' working tool.
- Beike shows that traffic alone does not fix renovation economics. Delivery quality and channel discipline matter more.

## Q5. India: offering, model and positioning (no financials)

### Takeaway
India's interiors market is dominated by vertically integrated "design-and-build" brands (Livspace, HomeLane/DesignCafe, Bonito, Arrivae, NoBroker Interiors, Square Yards' Interior Company). They sell modular interiors with headline promises such as 45–90 day delivery and 10-year warranties, and they own the whole customer relationship. ## Q6. Concrete lessons for firstbriq: verification, escrow and milestone payments, standardised quotes, warranties, reviews, monetisation

### Takeaway
The pattern that holds up across markets: strict, ongoing, published vetting (Checkatrade, Sweeten); money held by a regulated third party and released stage by stage after inspection (Tubatu, Sweeten Secure Pay); standardised, itemised quotes (Block, Kukun, Houzz AI estimates, Verified.RealEstate); a small, clear, honoured guarantee (Checkatrade £1,000, Thumbtack $1,000); and pros charged for outcomes or a predictable subscription, not for unqualified leads. What fails is venture-scale managed design with thin margins, platforms holding customer money themselves, and pay-per-lead models that send one lead to many pros.

### Cited Findings
- **Vetting**: Checkatrade runs up to 12 checks, continues checking after joining and expels members. Sweeten accepts fewer than 20% of applicants after in-person and jobsite review. BuildZoom scores contractors on public permit and licence history. — [Checkatrade](https://www.checkatrade.com/blog/trust/); [Dealroom on Sweeten](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/sweeten); [Independent on BuildZoom](https://www.independent.com/2016/10/06/buildzoom-connects-you-contractors/)
- **Escrow and milestones**: Tubatu releases bank-held funds stage by stage after homeowner confirmation and inspection, and freezes them on a complaint. Sweeten Secure Pay protects deposits. Tubatu's 2018 trouble over handling payments without a licence shows the regulatory risk. — [yzwb Oct 2026](https://www.yzwb.net/news/ch/202610/t20261010_401127.html); [Caplight on Sweeten](https://www.caplight.com/company/sweeten); [TMTPost 2018](https://www.tmtpost.com/3198888.html)
- **Standardised quotes**: Block bundled materials into negotiated labour quotes; Kukun prices by zip code in value, quality and luxury tiers; Houzz AI produces itemised labour and materials estimates; Verified.RealEstate issues one itemised quote per room. — [Inman](https://www.inman.com/2018/12/13/block-wants-to-take-the-guesswork-out-of-home-renovations/); [Business Wire on Kukun](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220309005629/en/Kukun-Launches-Next-Generation-Estimator-Product-Giving-Consumers-Unique-Insight-When-Budgeting-for-Home-Renovation-Costs); [Houzz Pro blog](https://pro.houzz.com/pro-learn/blog/houzz-pro-puts-the-power-of-artificial-intelligence-to-work-for-you); [VRE](https://interiors-vre.centralindia.cloudapp.azure.com/services/full-home)
- **Guarantees**: Checkatrade up to £1,000 for 12 months; Thumbtack up to $1,000 money back and $100,000 property damage, with hiring through the platform required; Angi's guarantee is under class-action challenge. — [Checkatrade](https://www.checkatrade.com/blog/trust/); [Thumbtack](https://thumbtack.com/guarantee); [TINA](https://truthinadvertising.org/class-action/angis-happiness-guarantee/?pg=3)
- **Monetisation options seen**: pros pay per lead (Thumbtack, Bark, MyBuilder per shortlist); pros pay a subscription (Checkatrade, hipages; hipages FY25 EBITDA margin 24%); success fee per transaction (Sweeten 1.75–3.75%; BuildZoom about 2.5%); designer SaaS ($55–$130 per user per month); advertising (Houzz from $499 a month); homeowner membership (Thumbtack Plus); product commission (Havenly, Livspace private labels); financing (Hearth, RenoFi); data licensing (BuildZoom, Kukun white-label). — sources as cited in Q1–Q2 above.
- **Failure modes**: Modsy (deal fell through, abrupt shutdown), Homepolish (unpaid designers, bank had first claim on cash), Laurel & Wolf (fulfilment failures and one-star reviews). — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2022/06/29/modsy-an-interior-design-startup-founded-by-a-google-ventures-partner-is-shutting-down-its-design-business-and-splitting-ties-with-designers/); [Business of Home](https://businessofhome.com/articles/homepolish-has-collapsed-designers-are-reeling); [Failory](https://www.failory.com/cemetery/laurel-wolf)
- **Lead-quality backlash**: Angi's network revenue fell 79% in Q4 2025 after it stopped sending each request to many pros. Bark faces complaints about credit expiry and refusal of refunds. — [Angi IR](https://ir.angi.com/node/13006/html); [ProductReview](https://www.productreview.com.au/listings/barkcom)

### Inferences (suggested lessons for firstbriq; analysis, not sourced fact)
1. **Publish the vetting.** List firstbriq's checks (ID, GST/company registration, site visit to completed projects, client references) on each profile, re-check every year, and publish removals, as Checkatrade does. Show an acceptance rate, as Sweeten does ("fewer than X% accepted"), as a trust signal.
2. **Escrow with a regulated partner, released after inspection.** Copy Tubatu: the homeowner pays into a bank or regulated escrow; firstbriq releases each milestone after photo evidence (and optionally a third-party inspection at key stages: electrical, plumbing, waterproofing, carcass install); the payment for a stage is frozen when a dispute is raised. Do not hold the money on firstbriq's own balance sheet.
3. **Standard quote template.** Require designers to quote on a common structure (rooms, then line items, material brand and grade, labour, finish tier) so Studio's quote comparison compares like with like. Show a typical cost range for Bengaluru by tier, in the manner of Kukun.
4. **A small, clear, platform-backed guarantee.** For example, firstbriq covers up to a fixed rupee amount when a milestone is not delivered as agreed, only for projects paid through firstbriq escrow. This also stops homeowner and designer from moving the deal off the platform (Thumbtack and Angi both tie coverage to hiring through them).
5. **Reviews tied to verified, paid projects.** Reviews only from escrow-verified projects, with AI summaries that cite specific milestones. This counters the self-selected, unverified reviews that dominate Trustpilot-style sentiment about Indian incumbents.
6. **Monetisation.** Avoid selling the same lead to many designers. Better options: a success fee on escrowed project value (Sweeten-style, low single-digit %), plus optional designer SaaS (Studio for pros) and later financing (Hearth-style no-cost EMI through partners). A paid listing subscription could follow once lead quality is proven (the hipages and Checkatrade path).
7. **Don't become a managed design firm.** Modsy, Laurel & Wolf and Homepolish show the margin and operational risk. Livspace and HomeLane already occupy the integrated position. firstbriq's defensible ground is neutral trust infrastructure for independent designers.
8. **Watch My Interior Designers** (same city, same message, launched May 2026) and **Verified.RealEstate** (same trust feature set, managed model).

### Gaps
- Legal and regulatory requirements for escrow and payment-aggregator setups in India (RBI rules) were not researched.
- No data on the take rates Indian designers would accept, or on designer-side SaaS willingness to pay in India.
- No independent measure of how often platform guarantees pay out (Thumbtack, Checkatrade).
